Physiotherapy at Home in Lucknow: Who Needs It, What a Session Includes and How to Choose

SM
Author Srinika Medisurge Medical Team (Guided by Dr. Piyush Saxena) Srinika Medisurge Doorstep Healthcare, Gomti Nagar, Lucknow Medically reviewed by Dr. Piyush Saxena

For many patients the hardest part of physiotherapy is getting to the clinic. After a stroke, a hip or knee surgery, or a long illness, travelling can be painful, tiring and risky. Physiotherapy at home brings the therapist to the patient, and lets the family learn the exercises in the place where the patient actually lives and moves.

Who benefits from physiotherapy at home?

  • Stroke and paralysis: to regain movement, balance and walking, and to prevent stiff joints. Starting early under guidance matters.
  • After knee, hip or spine surgery: to restore movement and strength step by step, as the surgeon allows.
  • Fractures and orthopaedic injuries: once the doctor permits movement, to rebuild strength and range.
  • Back, neck and joint pain: posture correction and a safe exercise plan.
  • Elderly patients with weakness or fear of falling: balance training and strength work.
  • Bedridden patients: gentle movements and positioning to prevent stiffness, bedsores and chest congestion.

What happens in a home physiotherapy session?

  1. Assessment: the physiotherapist asks about the diagnosis, surgery or stroke, reads the reports and checks movement, strength, balance and walking.
  2. A plan with goals: for example sitting without support, standing from a chair, or walking to the bathroom, so progress can be seen.
  3. Treatment: hands-on techniques and exercises suited to the patient. Intensity is raised slowly.
  4. Teaching the family: how to help the patient move safely, how to position the weak side, and which exercises to repeat between visits.
  5. Review: the therapist notes progress and adjusts the plan, and tells the doctor if something worries them.

Our home physiotherapy is led by Dr. Desh Deepak Singh (BPT, MPT Neuro), who works with stroke and neurological patients. You can read how the service works on the physiotherapy at home page.

How many sessions will be needed?

There is no single answer. It depends on the condition, how long ago it started, the patient's general health and how well the family continues the exercises between visits. After the first assessment the physiotherapist can tell you how often to visit and how long to continue, and will review this as the patient improves. Be careful with anyone who promises a fixed cure in a fixed number of days.

How to choose a home physiotherapist

  • Ask for the qualification (BPT or MPT) and the experience with your patient's condition, such as stroke or joint replacement.
  • They should assess before treating, set clear goals and explain the plan to you.
  • They should teach the family and give a home exercise plan, not only treat during the visit.
  • They should coordinate with the treating doctor, especially after surgery or in heart, lung or diabetic patients.
  • They should be honest about expected progress and never promise a guaranteed cure.

Stop and call the doctor if

  • The patient has chest pain, severe breathlessness, dizziness or fainting during exercise.
  • There is sudden new weakness, facial droop or trouble speaking.
  • One calf becomes swollen, red or painful.
  • Pain suddenly gets much worse or a surgical wound opens or oozes.

For recovery after a stroke or surgery, you may also like our guides on the first weeks of stroke care at home and home physiotherapy after stroke or surgery.

Important Medical Disclaimer

This guide is for general education and does not replace a doctor's examination. If the patient has severe breathlessness, chest pain, heavy bleeding, fainting, confusion or a seizure, call the national emergency number 108 or go to the nearest hospital emergency department. Do not stop or change medicines without asking your doctor.
